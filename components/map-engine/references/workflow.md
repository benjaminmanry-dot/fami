# Practical map production

Choose the work that improves the actual map. These are craft tools, not an ordered gate sequence.

## Spatial authorship

Start with the site's use, entry, exits, circulation, sightlines, and consequential features. Compose rooms in their connected context; an isolated beautiful room can still fail the map. Identify what occupants do and place recognizable work surfaces, seats, storage, waste, tools, access, and objects accordingly. Do not add every example to every room. Empty, abandoned, ceremonial, and monumental spaces need their own convincing logic.

Read box text and keys against the planned map: named physical features, cover, hazards, manipulable objects, passages, and transitions must exist where play expects them. Do not draw a false passage or let a label conceal impossible construction. If a reader adds map-region navigation, keep its DM-facing affordance separate from the player plate: do not let automatic area badges turn an intentionally unlabelled player map into a keyed one. One static fictional state suffices unless Ben requested transformations.

## Construction choices

Use coherent reusable families or author the necessary pieces. Check projection, scale, alpha margins, base, light direction, contact shadows, and shared depth before assembling assets. A baked plinth/shadow can make a finished prop unsuitable as a wall, stair, or connector. Edit clean source assets into compatible parts or use integrated construction; raw overlap does not create a plausible joint.

For substantial modular assemblies, the existing asset audit and construction graph templates can specify endpoints, shared planes, elevations, materials, occlusion, and ownership. Load their schemas when using that format. They are not a requirement to model every cup or weld chairs to a table. Integrated drawing can demonstrate construction directly.

A quick unlabeled blockout helps when route/elevation geometry is uncertain. A color/value sketch helps when composition or medium is uncertain. Choose either, both, or direct production according to the risk; they need no automatic independent report, hash-freeze, or human microgate. Inspect enough early to avoid an expensive preventable failure.

## Finish and quality

Develop a deliberate medium and hierarchy using `house-style-profile.v1.json` and the relevant actual anchors. Bright expressive color, authored material, coherent edges, and purposeful richness must survive at practical play scale. Avoid equal-detail collage and blank primitive diagrams. Ordinary furnishings and joined spaces should make the site feel used, not like an exhibition of isolated machines.

Construction legibility and aesthetic quality are both required in the actual artifact. Inspect whole-map composition, room/activity groups, object readability, and native detail. Use `cold-composition-review.md` for one independent review; the adventure cold reviewer may do this work when capable. A technically valid source graph cannot rescue unreadable or unattractive pixels.

## Revision and handoff

Retain original candidate identities and findings. After a correction, identify what changed and recheck affected geometry, feature correspondence, finish, and lineage. Broaden only if the correction invalidates broader evidence. Never call a targeted recheck another blind first pass.

Record actual resource use against Ben's whole-job budget and explicit artifact stops. Rebalance an agent-selected suballocation when needed; do not infer new spend, calls, or renewal. Hand back the complete commissioned map and supporting files, or an honest bounded failure with recovery and the remaining obstacle.

For private WIP, use the separate DRAFT-ONLY identity record in `release-manifest-contract.md`. For new complete maps, use its concise v1.4 final evidence and exact Ben acceptance route. Existing strict v1.3 records retain their contract and validator behavior. No record or label alone establishes artistic quality or Ben's acceptance.
