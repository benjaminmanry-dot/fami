# Independent actual-map review

Use one capable independent reviewer for the actual map. The same reviewer may cover the complete adventure when it can inspect the reader and images. Construction, tactics, lineage, native-scale finish, and beauty are distinct claims, not a requirement for five roles or files. Add a specialist only for an explicit material uncertainty outside the reviewer's competence.

## First encounter

Give a fresh reviewer the unlabeled player map and grid/scale statement, without lore, intended feature names, production explanations, prior verdicts, or defect history. Record the exact file identity, reviewer, and supplied context. Inspect at practical play scale and native resolution. Save this first report before supplying source/key context.

Useful prompt:

> Inspect this player battlemap as a place you must navigate. Trace routes and connections, boundaries and openings, distinct elevations and visible transitions. Identify recognizable objects and room/activity groups and what their arrangement suggests people do here. Cite visible regions for ambiguous passages, dark merged forms, floating or colliding elements, contradictory joins, false stairs, or texture defects. Distinguish observation from speculation. Name material failures and needed corrections; do not invent lore or a furniture quota.

## Compare against intended use and style

After the first findings, supply the relevant manuscript/key, scale requirements, and appropriate house-style anchors with neutral comparison roles. Check named usable features, starts/exits, hazards, routes, clearances, elevation transitions, and load-bearing mechanics against what is actually visible. A semantic explanation cannot rescue a false route or unreadable pile.

Compare whole composition, room use, recognizable objects, physical joins, material/edge/light coherence, palette/value hierarchy, intentional print marks, and richness with quiet circulation. Compare practical object scale, not merely equal image dimensions. If benchmark grid scale is unknown, say so. Sparse ritual anchors do not establish a furnished-room ceiling; inspection-only inspiration grants no pixel-use rights.

Useful prompt:

> Does this map meet the intended vivid retro pop-art target and convincingly depict this complete place? Identify concrete differences from the relevant anchors in composition, inhabited use, legibility, material richness, and mark language. Darkness, glow, generic texture, or detail quantity cannot substitute for those qualities. Report the material failure and a bounded correction, or no material problem. State any unverified claim.

## Findings and revisions

Preserve the first report. Classify findings by their effect: hard spatial/semantic/identity/lineage failure; quality failure against the commissioned target; small advisory irregularity; or taste observation. A tiny plausible construction irregularity need not block finishing or create a human question unless Ben's exact artifact decision already controls it. Do not reinterpret an existing owner rejection.

After correction, inspect the defect and affected boundary. Do not require another blind pass or complete report suite merely because pixels changed; record the new identity and which earlier evidence remains applicable. Distinguish an independent first pass from an informed recheck.

Early blockouts and cheap color proofs may use this approach when they resolve meaningful uncertainty, but separate fresh roles and pre-finish reports are not universal prerequisites. Strict legacy v1.3 packaging has its own historical proof requirements; this review does not manufacture those records.

"Usable tonight" is an exact limited owner disposition, not professional acceptance. Hashes and component passes prove only their stated properties. Keep failed reports, rejected art, and owner decisions immutable and outside new production lineage.
