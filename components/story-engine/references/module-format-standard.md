# Native Table Reader Standard

This file defines the shape of the one DM-facing adventure manuscript and its private local 5e.tools presentation. It applies to standalone one-shots, bounded campaign episodes, and keyed sites. Scene craft lives in `scene-and-check-standard.md`; mechanics in `mechanical-floor.md`; taste in `ben-grade-gate.md`.

## Proven Product

The canonical table product is one authoritative manuscript compiled into the native local 5e.tools interface. The House of Second Measures reader proved the spine-first campaign form; the Sunless Citadel OSE reader proved the same form for a large keyed dungeon. Both succeeded because the interface added navigation, links, and maps without creating a second version of the adventure.

Never require Ben to cross-reference a module and separate running notes. If a quick-reference document seems necessary, the manuscript is not yet organized correctly. Repair the manuscript.

A PDF is an optional export of the same manuscript only when Ben explicitly asks for print or PDF. It is not the default table product and does not define Gate 2.

## Core Rule

The DM reads top to bottom and runs the game. The next useful fact appears where play creates the need for it.

Write a thorough mechanical and narrative spine, then trust the DM to adjudicate ordinary deviations. Do not enumerate a forest of “if the party does X” allowances. Explicitly rule only:

- a special or counterintuitive interaction;
- a load-bearing fact whose answer changes the adventure;
- a custom procedure, clock, object, hazard, or victory condition;
- a likely rules interaction that ordinary system rules and the scene facts cannot answer.

Refusal scripts, spell-by-spell menus, generic option lists, and portable “sideways” branches are not completeness. They are usually bloat.

## Reading Order

Use only the headings the adventure needs, in this functional order:

1. **Title and premise.** Level/system, runtime, party, one concrete paragraph saying where play starts, what is wrong, and what this session reaches.
2. **Table control, if genuinely global.** Only current continuity, a real clock, the intended route through a large keyed site, or a compression rule that affects the whole session. Omit generic GM advice.
3. **Opening.** The first words or situation, the first meaningful decision, and the immediate pressure.
4. **Site or scene overview.** Put complete readable maps, residents, routes, and global site procedures before the first keyed area that depends on them.
5. **Scenes or keyed areas.** Present them in likely play or traversal order. Each entry carries its own visible situation, mechanics, consequences, and exits.
6. **Climax and aftermath.** State the objective, what changes, rewards, and the handoff back to the campaign.
7. **Appendices only when lookup is genuinely easier there.** Custom creatures, handout text, or a compact rules roster may live here. Ordinary scene logic may not.

Do not force separate Adventure Summary, Background, Dramatis Personae, Running the Adventure, route cards, clue trackers, emergency improv tables, or repeated “If Time Is Short” blocks. Include their useful facts once, at the earliest point where the DM needs them.

**Entry continuity.** Before play begins, the reader must make clear where play starts, what has already happened, why the first presented NPC or place matters now, and the available way or ways onward. For a campaign episode, use only the latest approved entry facts: do not replay completed events or invent unknown continuity. Put this connecting tissue with the opening and first-use material, not in a detached recap.

## First-Use Adjacency

An aside interrupts the module and must justify that interruption.

- Put an NPC's usable dialogue, wants, tells, and next move where the party first meets that NPC.
- Put an object's or custom rule's complete table procedure where the party first meaningfully interacts with it.
- Put a consequence beside the action that causes it.
- Put a clue beside the feature, witness, or search that reveals it.
- Put treasure at the point of discovery.
- Put a route link at the exit or decision that uses it.

Create a detached sidebar or global procedure only when the same rule governs several separated areas and the DM benefits from one stable reference. Introduce it before the first dependent area and link back to it at later uses. Never create a sidebar merely because a fact is interesting.

## Entry Shape

A scene or keyed area usually needs:

- optional short read-aloud or a concrete threshold description;
- one paragraph explaining what is happening now;
- the important people, objects, routes, and pressures in the order players encounter them;
- checks and custom mechanics beside their triggers, including the consequence and continuation;
- the resulting change and the next route or decision.

Use subheadings such as **Development**, **Treasure**, or **Exits** only when they make that particular entry faster to scan. Do not stamp the same furniture onto every room. Boxed text is optional for minor connective areas and required only when it gives the DM a useful opening image.

### Keyed-area correspondence

For a dungeon room, author in this order: **perceptible opening → named dungeon features with local behavior → physical map features**. The opening names only what the characters can perceive; a useful feature heading reuses a recognizable noun from that opening and carries its interaction, check, consequence, or route. The map then shows the same feature as physical fact. Keep secrets in the DM procedure until discovered, and do not give every noun a heading.

When a room has one dominant asset, author the space around it: approach, working area, sightline, boundary, route, or pressure that makes its use and occupancy intelligible. Do not hide an empty room behind descriptive prose or decorative clutter, and do not force a prop quota or the same feature pattern into every room.

Combat text answers three questions: why fighting starts or can be avoided; where opponents begin and how they pursue their objective; and what surrender, flight, victory, or defeat changes. Keep design math in the brief.

## Maps And Links

Maps are part of the reader, not an appendix afterthought.

- Put every complete map immediately before the first key that uses it.
- Show it at a readable width and preserve native detail.
- Give keyed regions working links to their entries and give entries links back to the relevant map or adjacent area when useful.
- State player start, meaningful routes, bottlenecks, hazards, objective zones, and state changes in the nearby prose.
- Record exact map paths and hashes. Accepted readers use accepted map identities. Private review may use new complete v1.4 candidates, strict legacy v1.3 review-admitted candidates, or the separate DRAFT-ONLY route for unfinished work with visible draft status and recorded missing checks. None grants acceptance or live-use permission; rejected or contaminated art cannot enter through a new format or draft label.

Link creatures, spells, items, conditions, handouts, global procedures, and keyed areas at the point of use. Validate every internal link and every source reference in the actual local reader. Source Markdown alone is not proof.

## Density And Hierarchy

Every paragraph earns its place through table procedure, concrete feeling, clue, consequence, or navigation.

Cut:

- repeated premise or continuity;
- lore that never changes play;
- a second explanation of a procedure already linked;
- exhaustive hypothetical branches;
- generic advice Ben already supplies as DM;
- tables that turn prose into a worksheet;
- separate quick-reference material that duplicates the manuscript.

Keep quiet space in the information hierarchy. One opening, one central pressure, and one climax should dominate. Supporting detail remains subordinate. Tables are islands for real lookups—clocks, encounter rolls, treasure, or compact comparisons—not the river of the adventure.

When two versions are runnable, the shorter one ships.

## Cold Reader Gate

The Cold DM begins in the actual local 5e.tools interface with no brief, production notes, or prior conversation. Reading top to bottom, the reviewer must be able to:

- start play inside ten minutes;
- find the next beat without hunting across documents;
- understand each custom procedure before first use;
- follow maps, area links, rules links, and exits;
- run the likely route and the climax without inventing a missing rule;
- distinguish a genuine missing spine fact from an ordinary deviation a competent DM can adjudicate.

The reviewer does not penalize the manuscript for omitting refusal scripts, exhaustive spell counterplay, or unlisted player improvisation when the scene facts and normal rules are sufficient.

## Optional PDF Export

When Ben explicitly asks for a PDF, export this exact manuscript. Do not add a separate running-notes appendix or rewrite it into a different product.

- Keep the same reading order and headings.
- Inspect every page at readable size.
- Avoid orphaned headings, split read-aloud/procedure, broken tables, blank pages, and unreadable maps.
- A contents page is useful for long exports, but the native reader remains the table authority.
- Original public-facing exports must not copy official logos, branding, fonts, ornament, or trade dress.

The export passes only when it preserves the already-approved reader. Layout cannot rescue a bloated or poorly ordered manuscript.
