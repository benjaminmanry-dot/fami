# Story Engine

Make an adventure Ben wants to run at a paid table: specific, strange, emotionally pressured, mechanically complete, and immediately understandable to a cold DM. The finished product is one authoritative manuscript in the owning project's private native 5e.tools reader, with the maps, links, and requested assets that let the DM run it from top to bottom. A successful build or impressive premise does not establish that quality.

## Own the adventure

Retrieve the current commission, approved structure, party/system/runtime, table evidence, and constraints. Reuse Ben's applicable decisions. Structure and final acceptance remain his decisions; do not ask him again because a task restarted or the owner switched skills. When direction is genuinely unsettled, explore materially different structures and bring him a concrete recommendation. The requested deliverable may itself be a concept or outline; do not expand it into full production.

Standalone work belongs in `C:\Ambitions\story-engine\one-shots\<slug>\`. Campaign episodes belong in their campaign's established adventure/prep location. Read that project's instructions and approved entry facts; preserve its continuity and handback. Transcripts and recaps are evidence, not permission to promote canon.

Keep the useful brief, design decisions, unresolved facts, resources, and deferrals in the existing adventure records. Templates are aids, not a requirement to fill irrelevant fields. The owner chooses an effective sequence and may develop text, spatial design, and reader together. Preserve Ben's whole-job budgets and explicit stops; adjust agent-selected suballocations within them when evidence warrants.

## Craft the playable spine

Read `references/ben-grade-gate.md` for Ben's recorded taste. Its quoted decisions remain evidence; inherited fixed pass orders, scene counts, and paperwork are not production obligations. Use the current workflow and review guidance here. Load `references/structure-patterns.md` when choosing structure and `references/realms-grounding.md` when setting matters. Read `references/scene-and-check-standard.md`, `references/module-format-standard.md`, and `references/mechanical-floor.md` for drafting and affected revisions.

Give the DM an inviting opening, the situation already in motion, people who want incompatible things, routes onward, and consequences that preserve player choices. In keyed areas, carry **box text → named usable features → visible map features**. Keep secrets out of read-aloud. A place should feel occupied or formerly used: approaches, working space, furnishings, storage, boundaries, and traces explain what happens there. Purposeful emptiness needs no furniture quota.

Supply recoverable, evocative situations: after a plausible unscripted departure, the changed fiction still gives the table something meaningful to investigate or act on while the DM regains bearings. Do not restore a planned sequence or write exhaustive contingency menus. State custom and counterintuitive mechanics, load-bearing exceptions, consequences, and victory conditions at first use. Ordinary rules and concrete scene facts support ordinary improvisation. Ben's observed table performance outranks encounter-budget arithmetic.

The manuscript is the table product. Keep design math, QA, prompts, and pipeline vocabulary outside it. Place NPC speech with the NPC, rules with their trigger, and readable maps before dependent keys. If a reader offers map-region navigation, keep it from adding unwanted key labels to an intentionally unlabelled player plate. Never create separate running notes to compensate for a poorly organized manuscript. One static fictional map is sufficient unless Ben explicitly requests variants; player/grid/GM derivatives are the same fiction.

## Build and prove the actual reader

Reuse the project-local reader builder; preserve manuscript meaning and source/output identity. Open the actual interface in Chrome under the workspace browser rules, follow the opening, maps, links, climax, and ending, and correct missing content or broken navigation. The production owner performs these checks and may assess its own work honestly; an independent review supplies different evidence.

Use one capable fresh-context reviewer for the actual-artifact cold run and relevant craft, rules, continuity, and map claims, following `references/qa-panel.md`. Add a specialist only for a concrete risk that this reviewer cannot competently resolve. No compulsory three-model panel, fresh role per phase, or full rerun after a local correction. Preserve the original findings and recheck the defect and affected boundary.

Private work-in-progress readers are normal production work within an authorized adventure. Label them **DRAFT-ONLY**, state missing assets and unperformed checks plainly, and continue toward the complete outcome. For unfinished maps use Map Engine's separate draft-only evidence route in `references/release-manifest-contract.md`; do not disguise rejected or contaminated art as a draft. A draft can support an honest review without passing final QA. It is neither Ben acceptance nor live-use permission.

Present the complete reader with exact evidence and remaining uncertainty for Ben's final decision. New complete maps use Map Engine v1.4 final evidence (one independent actual-artifact report and exact identities); legacy graph/pre-finish/report-count machinery is not needed. Only Ben's acceptance makes the reader accepted. An emergency "usable tonight" disposition retains its limits. Do not lower the target or change a validator to turn failed art into quality.
