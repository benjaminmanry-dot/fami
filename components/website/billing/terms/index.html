Player Billing

The flexible part stays human.

Stripe handles the card details. Ben still reviews the session, the amount, and what makes sense for you before anything is collected.

What saving a card means

You are authorizing future, variable session charges.

When you save a card through the private Stripe link Ben sends you, you authorize 20Fates to charge that saved card for your D&D sessions. The amount is based on the actual session duration or your reserved seat under the attendance policy, your privately agreed hourly rate, and any adjustment Ben approves.

Your normal timing will be agreed with you—usually after each weekly game, or as a monthly collection. The authorization continues until you revoke it.

How the amount is decided

After a game, Ben records the real session length and checks the calculation at your agreed hourly rate. He can reduce, defer, partially collect, or waive the amount before confirming anything.

Your personal rate stays private. It is not published, labeled as a discount, or compared with anyone else’s.

When collection happens

Most players are collected weekly after the game. Some pay monthly. Stripe does not make that decision and does not automatically choose what to charge.

There are no automatic late fees, repeated payment retries, or table consequences built into this system. If something needs time or a conversation, that remains between you and Ben.

What Stripe keeps

Stripe—not 20Fates—stores your complete card information. 20Fates sees only the Stripe customer record and a masked description such as the card type and last four digits.

Each collection creates an itemized Stripe record showing the campaign, date, duration, agreed rate, and final amount.

Changing your mind

You may revoke permission for future automatic charges at any time by messaging Ben before the next planned collection. Future automatic collection will stop once the revocation is received.

Revoking authorization does not erase an existing balance, but any outstanding amount will be handled through the same direct, case-by-case conversation as everything else.

Questions or changes

Talk to Ben directly.

If your rate, schedule, payment timing, or circumstances change, say so. Payment is meant to be the last obstacle between you and a table—not the first.
