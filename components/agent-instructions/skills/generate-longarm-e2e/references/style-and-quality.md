# Longarm E2E Style And Quality

## the textile designer-Informed Principles

Use these as process and quality cues, not as permission to copy her protected patterns.

- Let the quilt lead. the textile designer's public Mexican Stars process starts by identifying natural curves and accentuating piecing, with the explicit principle that quilting should not compete with piecing: https://susanmanry.com/mexican-stars-quilt/
- Build motifs from relevant inspiration, then move, rotate, resize, and multiply parts until they form workable designs.
- Finish by combining parts into continuous-line stitching designs.
- Treat thread, density, and showiness as design decisions. Low-profile, evenly spaced, or concentric choices may be better when fabric or piecing is already busy.
- Think like Pro-Stitcher work: accurate alignment, row wrapping, offsetting, drift control, start/end handling, borders, corners, and effective areas matter as much as the drawing.
- Respect commercial pattern formats and copyright. the textile designer product pages list file formats such as SVG, PNG, ABC, DEF, PDF, and related machine formats, but those files and their designs are protected: https://susanmanry.com/product/sunflower-love-border-1/

## Professional E2E Characteristics

- Original motif language with a clear visual idea: botanical, feather, pearl, scroll, shell, geometric, modern ribbon, seasonal, juvenile, or novelty.
- Continuous left-to-right path that can repeat horizontally with a clean tangent and no visible hiccup at the seam.
- Start and end points on the same horizontal plane within machine-scale tolerance; a computerized longarm will show even small y mismatches as a step between repeats.
- A single ordered stitch stream: no disconnected subpaths, jump moves, or tie-off-dependent travel hidden inside the design.
- Object layouts must have a coherent route plan. When objects sit in a square, use a path that visits each object once and does not stitch back along connector travel.
- Row-to-row nesting with planned vertical interlock, usually aided by an alternate-row offset.
- Balanced density: avoid large dead zones, but do not pack details so tightly that the quilt becomes stiff.
- Graceful motion: longarm-friendly curves, few abrupt reversals, no jittery point clouds.
- Intentional crossings only. Self-crossings are acceptable for leaves, feathers, pearls, and curls when they look designed and stitch cleanly.
- Strong preview discipline: inspect one row, at least four horizontal repeats, and at least four nested rows.

## Professional Coordinate Set Characteristics

- Coordinated designs for borders, blocks, triangles, sashing, and focal areas should feel related without being identical.
- The focal geography may carry the full motif library; supporting geographies should carry selected motifs and omit others.
- Density should stay similar from geography to geography through comparable line spacing, echo count, motif scale, and negative-space rhythm.
- Border, block, and triangle designs should be drawn for their actual quilt geography, not cropped from one generic drawing.
- Shared connector grammar should tie the family together: vine, pearl chain, ribbon, echo, curl, feather, or geometric channel.
- Familiar motif recurrence plus intentional omissions creates unity without boredom.

## Artistic Bar

Do not deliver scaffold art as professional art. A technically valid path is only a first draft.

For professional, heirloom, coordinate, or the textile designer-quality requests, load `artistic-engine.md`, `professional-art-standard.md`, `composition-templates.md`, and `susan-manry-informed-motif-library.md` before drawing. The design must pass thumbnail scoring and an art-board stage before DXF. This is especially important for borders, blocks, triangles, sashing, focal blocks, and whole-quilt coordinate sets.

Before delivery, ask whether the design has:

- A recognizable motif grammar that would make sense in thread.
- Variation in scale, rotation, and spacing without looking random.
- Elegant connector travel that feels like part of the drawing.
- Negative space that is intentional, not accidental.
- Curves that are smooth and confident, with no clip-art stiffness.
- A rhythm that would complement common quilt tops rather than dominate them.

Revise until the answer is yes. If the motif still looks generic, childish, jagged, or auto-generated, say so and keep iterating.

## Disqualifiers

Reject or revise patterns that resemble the weak attached examples:

- Shaky novelty outlines that look traced from a rough icon rather than digitized for stitching.
- Too many tiny point-to-point wiggles along what should be smooth curves.
- Awkward, diagonal, or obvious travel lines through motif interiors.
- Repeated rows that align like rigid stripes instead of nesting.
- E2E repeats whose start and end points do not share the same y coordinate, even if the motif itself looks good.
- Designs that require the longarm to jump, stop, tie off, or restart to finish what should be continuous-line quilting.
- Square object layouts that hop randomly, retrace connector lines, or leave obvious dead travel.
- Coordinate sets where every support area contains every motif, making the quilt repetitive.
- Coordinate sets with large density shifts between borders, blocks, triangles, and sashing.
- Triangle or border designs that look like block designs cropped to fit.
- Central medallion or block designs presented as E2E without row logic.
- Start/end dots that technically align but visually crash into the next repeat.
- Thin row depth that cannot fill a quilt gracefully.
- Overly literal drawings where quilting becomes the star and fights the quilt top.

## Drafting Heuristics

- Start with a spine or rhythm line before adding motifs.
- Use echoed shapes to control fill and nesting.
- Use pearls or small curls sparingly to solve gaps, not as clutter.
- Make at least one upper and one lower excursion per repeat so rows interlock.
- Keep recognizable novelty subjects stylized and quilt-like. If the theme is ducks, flowers, cats, stars, or pumpkins, translate it into flowing quilting motifs rather than outline art.
- Choose a recommended row advance smaller than the full design height when rows are meant to nest.
- Use a 50% offset as the default, then tune it to avoid vertical channels and collisions.

## Delivery Notes

Include:

- Pattern name.
- Repeat width, design bounds, row advance, alternate-row offset, stitch spacing, and point count.
- Editable DXF handoff file.
- Preview images, SVGs, point lists, or other source artifacts only when the user asks for QA extras.
- A reminder to machine-test before customer release.
