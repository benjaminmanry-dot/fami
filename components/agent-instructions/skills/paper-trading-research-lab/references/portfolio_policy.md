# Portfolio Policy

## Core Idea

A strategy is more valuable when it improves the whole paper-trading portfolio, not merely when it has attractive standalone metrics.

## Marginal Contribution Checks

For every candidate that survives standalone validation, measure:

- correlation to existing strategies, benchmark, and major risk factors
- drawdown overlap with existing strategies
- behavior during crisis, high-volatility, low-volatility, trend, and chop regimes
- marginal Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar, and drawdown contribution
- turnover interaction and execution timing conflicts
- gross and net exposure impact
- capital efficiency and capacity at current and future paper capital
- tail-risk contribution and worst-path contribution

## Candidate Actions

- Reject: weak standalone evidence or duplicates existing risk without improving the portfolio.
- Research more: plausible edge but incomplete robustness, cost, or data evidence.
- Incubate: passes standalone validation and has potential portfolio value.
- Promote to review: paper results remain aligned with OOS expectation and portfolio contribution is positive.
- Pause: formerly acceptable candidate deteriorates, crowds, correlates, or becomes operationally unreliable.

## Diversification Preference

Prefer candidates that add a genuinely different source of return:

- different edge family
- different holding period
- different asset class or universe
- different market regime profile
- different execution schedule
- lower drawdown overlap

Do not over-allocate to many variants of the same signal. Treat highly correlated variants as one research family for trial counting and risk.

## Portfolio Report Additions

When comparing candidates, include:

- standalone verdict
- marginal portfolio verdict
- correlation and drawdown-overlap summary
- capital allocation impact
- risk budget consumed
- execution conflicts
- recommended queue order for paper incubation
