# Thrones Of The Djinni Lords Style Guide

Use this for the normal Thrones workflow. Do not include Gloomwrought or Intrigue material unless the user explicitly asks.

## Core Feel

Thrones of the Djinni Lords is about elemental courts, impossible desert grandeur, dangerous bargains, diplomacy, tomb-raiding, high-magic politics, and desert survival.

## Table Texture

Use:

- Brass, lapis, blue tile, silk shade, glass sand, carved wind, hot stone, oath cups, water ledgers, mirrored dunes, and cool impossible shadow.
- Djinni hospitality as a threat: beautiful, precise, generous, binding.
- Water-rights as survival, law, and leverage.
- Oath-debt as treasure, trap, and political currency.
- Tombs as playable spaces with choices, locks, rivals, witnesses, and costs.
- Elemental magic as protocol, environment, and legal force.

## Pressure Sources

- A bargain has a hidden interpretation.
- A court invitation cannot be casually refused.
- A rival reaches a witness first.
- A water route changes price.
- A relic proves more than ownership.
- An ifrit retainer identifies a loophole.
- Local survival conflicts with court prestige.

## Playable Court Mechanics

Turn style into table procedures:

- Hospitality creates jurisdiction.
- Water-rights require witnesses.
- Promises can open doors, alter terrain, or summon debt.
- Court shade can define where a rule applies.
- A witness mark can matter more than physical possession.
- A relic can be evidence before it is treasure.

## Route Framing

Thrones route choices should usually ask which cost the party accepts:

- Speed at the price of water or injury.
- Safety at the price of obligation.
- Clarity at the price of broker control.
- Defiance at the price of court attention.

## Avoid

- Generic desert bandits without a faction reason.
- Lore lectures without immediate table decisions.
- Treasure that is only treasure.
- Djinni who are merely wish vendors.
- Tomb rooms that only explain history.

## Useful Phrase Bank

- "The invitation is hospitality, and hospitality is jurisdiction."
- "The door weighs promises, not stone."
- "Water is not bought here. It is witnessed."
- "The relic is evidence before it is treasure."
- "The court smiles because the cost has not yet been named."
