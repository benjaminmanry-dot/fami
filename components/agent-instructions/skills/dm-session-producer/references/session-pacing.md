# Session Pacing Reference

Use this when preparing a 4-hour D&D session. Treat the structure as a pressure budget, not a railroad.

## 4-Hour Structure

| Time | Function | Output At Table |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:15 | Re-entry and immediate choice | Recap, table orientation, visible next decision |
| 0:15-1:05 | Exploration or approach | Travel, investigation, scouting, resource pressure |
| 1:05-1:55 | Roleplay or negotiation | NPC agendas, bargains, faction pressure, social stakes |
| 1:55-3:10 | Combat, hazard, or crisis | Tactical pressure with story consequences |
| 3:10-3:40 | Fallout and branch resolution | Consequences, clues, rewards, new obligations |
| 3:40-4:00 | Landing beat | Cliffhanger, next-session choice, player-facing recap hook |

## Balance Rules

- Give every hour at least one meaningful choice, one sensory anchor, and one consequence.
- Rotate play modes. Avoid two long lore scenes in a row.
- Let combat or crisis express the politics, bargain, terrain, or survival problem already in play.
- Keep exposition attached to an action: a door, deal, witness, map, threat, or cost.
- Give social scenes failure states beyond "no progress": debt, insult, rumor, obligation, delay, or rival advantage.
- Make exploration interactive with tools, thresholds, hazards, partial maps, false safety, or resource strain.

## Route Design

A good 4-hour prep packet should present at least three routes that are all playable:

- Direct route: fastest path to the obvious pressure.
- Information route: slower path that buys clarity, leverage, or safety.
- Relationship route: route through an NPC, faction, debt, or promise.
- Elsewhere route: portable pressure that follows the players without forcing them back.

For each route, define:

- Cost: time, water, debt, danger, insult, exposure, or rival advantage.
- Reward: clue, position, witness, safety, ally, or leverage.
- Consequence: what changes if they take it or ignore it.

## Pacing Nutrition

Every prepared session should include:

- A clear opening pressure.
- A faction or NPC who wants something now.
- A tangible place the players can manipulate.
- A secret or clue that changes decisions.
- A cost for hesitation.
- A reward that creates a future problem.
- A branch for players who go elsewhere.

## Minimum Viable Session

If prep time collapses, the DM should still be able to run:

- One opening choice.
- Three scene cards.
- Five secrets or clues.
- One pressure encounter or hazard.
- One NPC agenda table.
- One ending/cliffhanger.

## What If Players Go Elsewhere?

Prepare one portable pressure that can move to any location:

- An NPC arrives with a demand.
- A rival claims a visible advantage.
- A resource problem becomes immediate.
- A clue points to a different route.
- A faction reacts to the party's absence.

Do not punish wandering. Make elsewhere playable and let the world keep moving.
