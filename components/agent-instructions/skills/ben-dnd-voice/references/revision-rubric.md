# Revision Rubric

Use this after the first draft, especially for essays, Reddit posts, and advice pieces.

## Voice Audit

- Does the piece sound like a professional DM talking to other DMs, not a content marketer summarizing DM advice?
- Is the irritation aimed at bad advice or brittle habits, not at DMs who are learning?
- Does the draft use contractions naturally?
- Are non-contracted phrases reserved for emphasis, clarity, or a deliberate beat?
- Is there at least one practical distinction the reader can remember?
- Does the draft reflect patterns from `source-corpus.md` and `voice-fingerprint.md`, rather than generic Reddit advice voice?

## Salience Audit

- Does the post correct a live DM pain point?
- Does the central distinction change what the reader would prep, say, notice, or ask?
- Is the example specific enough to feel table-born?
- Does the post create a useful conversation rather than merely invite engagement?
- For outreach, does it serve the subreddit before it serves Ben?

## Rhythm Audit

- Merge excess one-sentence paragraphs. Single-line paragraphs should earn their place.
- Vary paragraph length without making the post dense.
- Add connective sentences where the draft jumps too abruptly from claim to claim.
- Preserve punch, but don't let punch replace thought.
- Read the opening aloud. It should sound like a pointed person, not a headline generator.

## Practicality Audit

- Add one vivid table example if the draft is mostly abstract.
- Make sure the example creates choices, pressure, consequences, or usable language.
- Replace generic fantasy references with table-specific details.
- If an NPC, town, clue, monster, or villain appears, give it a present-tense want, fear, scarcity, or next move.
- Turn the example into prompts or questions the DM can take to their own prep.

## Generosity Audit

- Add a softener early if the opening could read as ragebait.
- Include table variation: "This is not a moral law. It is a tool." or equivalent.
- Avoid pretending one structure works for all tables.
- End with DM agency, not a commandment.

## Common Fixes

If the draft sounds too AI-generated, do these in order:

1. Combine nearby one-line paragraphs into fuller paragraphs.
2. Replace formal phrasing with contractions and plainer speech.
3. Add one oddly specific table detail that has a job in the argument.
4. Cut any sentence that only labels the point instead of advancing it.
5. Make the final line useful and grounded instead of inspirational.

If the draft sounds too glib, add a real constraint, cost, or tradeoff.

If the draft sounds too soft, sharpen the opening claim and name the problem plainly.

If the draft sounds too absolute, add table permission and one case where the advice might not apply.
